# Final challenge: Making municipal decisions easier to understand

Confirmed on Devpost (banner, 13 Nov) and Discord. Full brief:
`briefs/FINAL_Municipal_Decisions_Challenge_Brief.docx` (same topic as
`briefs/CIVIC-Hack_brief-1_municipal-decisions.pdf`).

> How might we help residents understand a proposed municipal decision, how it
> could affect them, and when they can have a say?

## Success, per the brief

Take a real decision or staff report and show in plain language:
what is proposed and why · who/where it affects ("is this near me?") ·
key dates and how/when to have a say · a link to the source for every claim.
Tested on 2–3 real agenda items, accurate, neutral. A municipal leader can use
it without the team in the room.

## Hard constraints

- Distinguish staff recommendation vs committee decision vs final council decision.
- Show source and date for every explanation; link to the exact passage.
- Keep conditions and uncertainty. AI summaries must be checked against records.
  "A summary that mis-states a decision is worse than no summary."
- Verify participation info on the official item page. **Never infer a
  submission deadline from a meeting date.**
- Plain, accessible, non-partisan. Don't show personal info from correspondence.

## Starter data (City of Toronto Open Data)

- Report Request Log — https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/report-request-log/
- Council Voting Record — https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/members-of-toronto-city-council-voting-record/
- Any other public data allowed; cite sources and dates.

## Municipal leaders' advice (CUI Summit, Fri morning, via Discord)

- Make the visualizations clear. Focus on the visual.
- Keep it simple.
- Think pilot: pick one part ("a nugget") and do it well.

Laws they raised: Planning Act, AODA, duty to consult. Organizers say these are
a starting point, not a rulebook: check official sources for anything central to
the tool and state assumptions in the Municipal Readiness Report.
Full panel answers are in the Discord forum (not copied here).
